Most supply problems in this category are preventable at the quotation stage. The twelve questions below are the ones that actually change the outcome; everything else is logistics detail.

## Product definition

**1. What exactly is the pack format?**
Per vial, per kit, vials per kit, fill mass per vial. "10 mg" without a unit count is not a specification.

**2. Which salt form?**
Acetate, TFA, or something else. This changes the mass, the pH after reconstitution and the price per milligram of peptide.

**3. What is the net peptide content, not just the HPLC purity?**
These are different numbers. See [Purity vs Content](/peppedia/purity-vs-content).

## Analytical documentation

**4. Which HPLC method produced the purity figure?**
Column, gradient, wavelength, integration threshold. Reproducibility depends on all four.

**5. Is identity confirmed by mass spectrometry, with observed and theoretical mass?**
"Confirmed" without numbers is not confirmation.

**6. What are the water content and, where applicable, endotoxin results?**
Karl Fischer and LAL. These are the specifications that decide usability for cell work.

**7. Can you send the COA for the exact lot you will ship, before I pay?**
The single most discriminating question in the list.

## Traceability and compliance

**8. How is the lot numbered, and does the number appear on the vial label?**
Traceability requires the certificate and the physical unit to agree. Ask for the numbering scheme, not just an example.

**9. What end-use documentation can you provide?**
Certificate of analysis, MSDS, packing list, commercial invoice, and — for institutional buyers — an end-use or non-human-use statement.

**10. Are you the manufacturer or a reseller?**
Neither is disqualifying, but it changes who can answer questions 4–7 and how quickly.

## Commercial terms

**11. What are the tier breaks, incoterm and lead time from payment?**
Also ask what happens to documentation if a lot is split across two shipments.

**12. Can you support private label or a fixed-composition blend, and from what quantity?**
For distributors this is the difference between reselling and building a product line. Typical thresholds are around 50 kits per SKU.

## How to use the list

Do not send all twelve in the first email. Send four — pack format, salt form, COA availability, lead time — and see how the supplier responds. The answer tells you whether the remaining eight are worth asking.

A useful signal: suppliers who answer with a PDF and a lot number are usually manufacturing or holding stock. Suppliers who answer with adjectives are usually in the middle of a chain.

## Red flags in a quotation

- Price without pack format.
- "Purity 99%" with no method.
- No lot number until after payment.
- Refusal to send any COA before an order.
- Lead time quoted as "immediately" with no stock reference.

None of these are fatal on their own. Two or more together usually means the quote was assembled from someone else's price list.
